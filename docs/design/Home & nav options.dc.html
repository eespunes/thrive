How it behaves
Per person, not per family. Your board lives on your profile; the numbers inside come from the shared workspace, so nobody sees different truth.

Edit mode. Long-press any widget or tap the pencil: drag to reorder, × to remove, tap the size chip to cycle S → M → L where a widget supports it.

Every widget is a shortcut. Tapping the body opens the matching screen with the right filter; the small buttons act in place (tick a task, pay a bill, open a card).

Sensible defaults. New members start with Balance · Today · Tasks · Shopping, so an untouched home still looks finished.

Kids. Kid profiles get the kid-safe subset only (day, chores, pocket money, meals) — money widgets aren't offered.

Four ways to make the home screen earn its place

Today's home is a fixed stack of read-only cards, and the five-tab bar spends two slots on things most people open once a week. Each option below changes a different thing: what the home is made of, who decides, and how many tabs you actually need. Reply with an id (1a–1d) and I'll build it into the prototype.

Same home, but every card becomes a widget you can reorder, resize, remove or add — and the layout is saved per person, not per family. Smallest change, highest familiarity. Nav stays as it is.
